Your business may be growing. But are the numbers telling the same story?

A business can look busy from the outside. Sales are growing. Customers keep coming. The team is getting bigger. Yet many business owners still find themselves asking:

  • If the business is doing well, why does cash still feel tight?
  • Where is most of our money actually going?
  • Are we really profitable?
  • Which products or business units make the most money?
  • Are our operating costs becoming too high?
  • Can we afford to expand?
  • Why is our profit different from the cash in our bank account?
  • How much working capital do we actually need?

These are not just accounting questions. They are business decisions hidden inside financial numbers.

The “aha” moment

When a business owner finally understands the numbers

One of the most interesting moments in financial consulting is when an owner sees their financial statements differently for the first time.

A business owner I worked with knew how much the company was selling. But after we looked deeper into the financial statements, operating expenses, assets and cash flow, the picture became much clearer.

One area that created an “aha” moment was depreciation. The business had invested money into assets used over several years. The cash had already been spent when those assets were purchased. But accounting does not necessarily recognise the entire cost immediately in the profit and loss statement. Instead, part of that cost may be recognised gradually through depreciation.

That means: profit is not the same as cash flow.

And the bank balance does not tell the whole story of how a business is performing. Once the owner understood this, the financial statements stopped being “accounting documents”. They became a tool for understanding the business.

That is the kind of financial clarity I help business owners build.

FAQ

Questions owners usually ask first

Do I need complete financial statements before working with you?

Not necessarily. If your financial records are incomplete, the first step may simply be identifying what information already exists and what needs to be organised before deeper analysis can be done.

Is this the same as accounting?

No. Accounting primarily records and reports financial transactions. Financial advisory focuses on interpreting that information to understand profitability, cash flow, financial performance and business decisions.

Can you work with my existing accountant?

Yes. In many cases this is the ideal setup. Your accountant continues managing accounting records and compliance work while financial advisory focuses on management analysis and decision-making.

Can consultations be conducted in English?

Yes. Consultations for international clients can be conducted in English.

Do I need to be based in Bali?

No. The advisory process can also be conducted online, although this page is specifically designed for founders and business owners operating in Bali and Indonesia.

What type of business do you usually help?

The service is suitable for owner-led businesses where the founder wants greater financial visibility, particularly hospitality, property-related operations, F&B, professional services, creative businesses and other growing companies.
